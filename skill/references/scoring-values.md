# Empty Stadiums — Scoring Conversion and Position Values

**Verified against ESPN league settings on Aug 21, 2026.** Scope: draft day only. These are preseason projections and go stale once real games are played — for in-season lineup, waiver, and trade decisions, pull live production instead.

## The method, stated plainly

The edge here is **not** out-evaluating FantasyPros. Their ECR is a consensus of 120+ analysts and it will beat any solo evaluation on raw player assessment.

The edge is **arithmetic they can't do**, because they don't know this league. Take their stat projections and re-score them under Empty Stadiums rules. Same inputs, different scoreboard.

Do not freelance on player evaluation. Convert, don't second-guess.

**Two bonuses that used to exist were removed for 2026 and must not be reasoned from:** the 0.5-per-target bonus and the 0.05-per-completion bonus. Any analysis carrying those forward will overvalue high-target receivers, pass-catching backs, and high-volume accurate passers.

**What still makes this league unusual:** 6-point passing TDs (up from 4 in 2025), a superflex OP slot, 2.2 per solo tackle with two IDP starters, no kicker or defense, and return yards scored at full rushing rate.

**Quarterback replacement is a moving line, not a fixed rank.** A QB belongs in the OP slot exactly as long as the best available quarterback outscores the best available receiver or back — at some point that stops holding. That crossover *is* replacement level, landing around **295–305** in 2026, roughly QB22–24. Apply the rule, not the number.

## What the 2026 scoring change did — read this before applying old advice

Removing the target and completion bonuses stripped roughly **80 points from elite receivers**, **48 from pass-catching backs**, **65 from elite tight ends**, and **15–21 from high-volume quarterbacks**. Because those hits were uneven, the position hierarchy reordered:

1. **Running backs now outscore wide receivers at the elite level** — roughly 380 to 367, with a wider VORP gap (175 vs 157). The old "receiver over back at equal value" rule is **reversed**: at comparable value, **take the back**.
2. **The QB4–6 tier now beats elite receivers on VORP** (160 vs 157). A second quality quarterback is worth more than a third good receiver.
3. **Elite tight end value collapsed** from +138 VORP to +110, below IDP. Bowers and McBride are still worth having, but the TE cliff at pick 50 is no longer worth reaching for — let it come to you or take the position late.
4. **Return yards were untouched while everything else shrank**, so returners are now *relatively more* valuable than before. See below.
5. **Quarterback remains the top of the board by a wide margin** — the 6-point passing TD does that work, and it survived the change.

## Return production — the largest unpriced edge in this league

Kick and punt return yards score **0.1 each**, identical to rushing and receiving yards, and **return touchdowns are worth 10** — nearly double an offensive score. No national ranking includes any of it.

**The league is deliberately elevating this.** Two consecutive Aug 2026 rule changes both increased return value: first the target and completion bonuses were removed (shrinking every offensive total while returns were untouched), then return TDs went 6 → 10. Treat returner scoring as a standing priority rather than a quirk, and re-check these values before drafting in case the trend continues.

**What the TD change is worth:** a returner who scores twice gains 8 points outright, and it shifts preference toward **big-play returners over pure volume returners**. A specialist averaging 28 yards a return with breakaway speed is now worth more than one grinding out 22 with no house calls. Rashid Shaheed — the only player with both a punt and kick return TD in 2025 — gains the most from this specific change.

**The edge is growing.** The NFL moved touchbacks to the 35-yard line, and league-wide kickoff return rate jumped from 32.8% in 2024 to **74.5% in 2025**. Returns of 40+ yards went from 59 to 86 in one season.

|---|---|---|

| **Devin Duvernay (ARI)** — signed to handle both duties | — | ~100–150 if he holds both |
| **Ray Davis (BUF)** — led NFL at 30.4 yards per return | — | ~80–120 |

Return duty is worth **50 to 180 points**, and after the scoring change that's a *larger share* of a typical roster spot than it was. Turpin at ~210 total is genuinely flex-startable in a format where an elite receiver projects 367.

Two rules follow:

1. **In the last four rounds, a full-time dual returner outranks a generic upside flier.**
2. **When two late-round skill players are close, the one with return duty wins outright.**

**Variance note — dual-role vs pure specialist.** A returner who also collects receptions or carries has two independent income streams that smooth each other, which gives him a genuine weekly floor. Marvin Mims (Denver WR role plus punt returns) is that profile. A **pure specialist** like Turpin is boom/bust — 20 yards one week, 150 and a score the next. Both are worth drafting; only the dual-role type belongs anywhere near a lineup slot.

**Verify current duty before drafting** — return jobs change in camp and disappear after a muffed punt. Check the most recent preseason game, not last year's usage.

## Format mismatches — where reputation and value diverge

**Worth more than reputation:**

- **Elite running backs** — they now top every skill position on VORP, which is closer to national consensus than the old scoring was, but the *margin* over receivers is bigger here than anywhere else.
- **Full-time returners** — 50 to 180 points nobody else prices.
- **Three-down linebackers** — at 2.2 per solo tackle an elite LB projects ~326, which now *exceeds* an elite tight end. The IDP slots are not throwaways.
- **Volume passers generally** — 6-point passing TDs keep the whole position elevated.

**Worth less than reputation:**

- **Elite tight ends** — the target bonus was most of their edge. Bowers and McBride fell from +138 VORP to +110, below linebackers.
- **High-target, low-touchdown receivers** — slot-heavy profiles lost the most when the target bonus went away. Prefer receivers who score.
- **Pass-catching backs relative to workhorses** — the receiving premium shrank by roughly 48 points, which brings two-down runners like Derrick Henry back toward par. He's still limited by catching nothing, but the penalty is now ~13 points rather than ~48.

## Other 2026 league changes worth knowing

- **4 minutes per pick** (up from 2) — real time to think on the clock.
- **Continuous FAB waivers, processing six days a week** ($500 budget). The wire is live nearly every day, which strengthens every "waivers will cover it" assumption in the bye and risk rules.
- **No trade review and no veto** — trades process immediately. This materially improves the "draft talent, fix byes by trade" plan.
- **Playoffs are weeks 16 and 17, one-week rounds, 4 teams.** 15 regular-season matchups. No bye week can touch the playoffs.
