## What 2QB changes

This is the single most important fact about the league. With 24 starting QB slots across 12 teams, roughly every startable QB in the NFL is rostered. Consequences that should shape nearly every recommendation:

- **QB values inflate.** Don't apply 1QB trade charts or 1QB ADP — use SF columns (see `trade-values-2qb.md`).
- **Streaming barely exists.** By midseason the waiver wire has no usable QB. Backup QBs must be acquired *before* the bye weeks that force the issue, not during.
- **A third QB has real trade value** to a team whose starter got hurt. Rostering one is a legitimate asset play, not a wasted bench spot.
- **Elite RB/WR supply is tighter too** — 12 teams with a flex means replacement level is worse than in shallow leagues, so depth holds value longer than it would in Empty Stadiums.

## Waiver / FAAB workflow

When David asks who to bid on, he should supply (or the data pipeline should provide) his roster, remaining FAAB, and the available pool. Then:

1. **Find the real need.** Look past raw talent on the wire to where his lineup actually breaks: an upcoming bye stack, an injury, a starter with no viable backup. A great WR5 is worth less than a mediocre RB2 if RB is where he starts a zero.
2. **Score by rest-of-season delta, not name recognition.** The right comparison is the target's RoS projection minus the RoS projection of whoever he'd drop. A +47 point upgrade justifies real money; a +8 upgrade does not, regardless of how exciting the player looks.
3. **Bid by tier, as a share of remaining budget** — percentages rather than fixed dollars keeps the strategy correct in September and in December:
   - **Tier A** (RB1/WR1 upside, +50 RoS or better): 15–25%
   - **Tier B** (flex-worthy starter, +30–50): 8–15%
   - **Tier C** (depth or lottery ticket, +10–30): 3–7%
   - **Tier D** (handcuff, under +10): 1–3%
4. **Rank 3–5 targets in bid order** with an explicit fallback: bid A first, and if outbid, the money goes to B on Saturday. Deadlines are Wednesday and Saturday nights, so a losing bid isn't a lost week if the next target is already picked.
5. **Keep a reserve.** Spending down to near zero in October leaves him defenseless when a starting RB tears an ACL in November. Flag it when a recommended bid would drop his reserve below roughly a third of the original budget with a month or more left.

## Trade evaluation

Provide both rosters and the exact proposal. Method:

1. Look up every player in **both** charts in `trade-values-2qb.md` and use the **SF column**, never the 1QB column.
2. Average the two sources per player, sum each side, take the delta. Trades within about ±5 points are fair; beyond that the value is going somewhere.
3. Then adjust for context the charts can't see — this is where the real answer lives:
   - **Positional fit:** trading from surplus to need can justify losing raw value. Trading his QB2 in a 2QB league almost never can.
   - **Bye weeks:** does the deal create a week where he can't field a lineup?
   - **Playoff schedule:** weeks 15–17 matter more than the regular season.
   - **What the opponent gains:** a trade that fixes their only weakness is worse for David than the point delta suggests.
4. **Give a verdict** — accept, decline, or counter with the specific counter. "It's close" is only useful if it comes with a tiebreaker.

### The four facts that drive every recommendation

**1. QBs front-load, then the run reloads in rounds 4-5.** QB1-5 go at picks 1-5 in *both* years, automatically. Then the room pivots to skill (WR1 avg 6.5, RB1 avg 7.0) and QB6 goes 7.5 — but QB7 slides to 11.5. The pause is real but short: by pick 40 sixteen to seventeen QBs are gone, and by pick 57 it's twenty to twenty-three. **Do not plan to get a flat-band QB at pick 57.**

**2. Round-1 QB count is the live trigger.** 2024 took 9 in round 1 and 13 by pick 24. 2025 took 6 and 11. Count them at the draft:
- **13+ gone by pick 24** → hot room, pull QB1 up a round
- **11-12** → plan holds
- **10 or fewer** → you can push QB2 back a round

**3. TE is the largest exploitable edge in the league.** Only **10 tight ends (2024) and 7 (2025) were drafted in 120 picks** — for twelve starting slots. Half the league starts a waiver TE. TE1 goes around pick 35, then a **32-pick hole** before TE2 at 68. **You can wait until round 8 and still get a starter.** Never spend a round-3 pick on a tight end here.

**4. The QB3 trade play is a coin flip, not free money.** 2024 drafted exactly 24 QBs — zero teams carried a third, the wire was barren, and a QB3 was worth ~+112 to an injured team. 2025 drafted **31** — seven teams had a third, so no desperate buyer existed. Average 27.5. **Hold a QB3 because it costs nothing, but do not plan around its trade value.** (An earlier version of this file called it "free money" and a 12-13x return.
